Here are five well-known mathematical results, with their conditions stated explicitly. The variables $p,n,k$ are integers, $i^2=-1$, and $\\pi$ is the circle constant.

## Wilson’s theorem

For a prime $p$, $(p-1)!\equiv -1 \pmod p$. More precisely, an integer $m>1$ is prime if and only if $(m-1)!\equiv -1 \pmod m$. Here, $r!$ is the product of the integers from 1 through $r$.

## Euler’s formula and identity

For a real number $x$ measured in radians, Euler’s formula is $e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x$. Setting $x=\pi$ gives the special case known as Euler’s identity: $e^{i\pi}+1=0$.

## [Fermat’s Last Theorem](https://kenji.blog/en/p/fermats-last-theorem/)

For every integer $n\ge 3$, there are no positive integers $x,y,z$ satisfying $x^n+y^n=z^n$. The restrictions to positive integers and exponents at least 3 are essential.

## The Basel problem

The infinite sum of the reciprocals of all positive integer squares is $\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

## Euler’s prime-producing polynomial

The polynomial $n^2+n+41$ gives a prime for the integers $n=0,1,\ldots,39$. It does not do so for every integer: at $n=40$, it equals $1681=41^2$, which is composite.
