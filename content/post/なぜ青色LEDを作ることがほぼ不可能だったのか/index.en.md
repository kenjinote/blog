## Why Blue LEDs Were So Difficult: GaN and the 2014 Nobel Prize

Developing a blue LED was not simply a matter of changing the color of a red LED. Blue photons carry more energy than red photons, so the semiconductor needs a wider band gap. Gallium nitride (GaN) was promising, but growing high-quality crystals and achieving p-type conductivity were major challenges.

Isamu Akasaki, Hiroshi Amano, and Shuji Nakamura developed efficient blue LEDs and received the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics. The official citation says their invention enabled bright, energy-saving white light sources. “Nearly impossible” describes the scale of the challenge; it does not mean the task was literally impossible.
