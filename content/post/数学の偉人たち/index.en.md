Here are some of the great mathematicians who left their mark on history, introduced in order of their birth.

### Pythagoras (c. 582 BC - c. 496 BC)
An ancient Greek mathematician and philosopher. He championed the idea that "all is number" and founded the Pythagorean brotherhood. He is famous for the "Pythagorean theorem," which shows the relationship between the lengths of the sides of a right triangle, but legend also has it that the discovery of irrational numbers shook the very foundations of his brotherhood.

### Archimedes (c. 287 BC - 212 BC)
An ancient Greek mathematician, physicist, and inventor. He accurately calculated the approximate value of pi using the method of exhaustion and discovered the formulas for the volume and surface area of a sphere. He used methods that can be considered the dawn of calculus, and is regarded as the greatest mathematician of antiquity.

### Euclid (c. 3rd century BC)
A Greek mathematician in ancient Egypt. In his book "Elements," he established the "axiomatic system," in which theorems are logically derived starting from a small number of axioms, which had an immeasurable influence on the subsequent development of mathematics and science.

### Diophantus (c. 207 - c. 291)
A Greek mathematician. He is famous for his introduction of symbolism and his study of rational solutions. His masterpiece is "Arithmetica". The field of finding rational solutions to equations is called Diophantine analysis.

### L. Fibonacci (c. 1175 - c. 1250)
An Italian mathematician. He used Hindu-Arabic numerals in his main work "Liber Abaci" (The Book of Calculation) and worked to popularize them. He also discussed congruent numbers in "Liber Quadratorum" (The Book of Squares).

### C. G. Bachet (1581-1638)
A French nobleman who studied number puzzles as a hobby. He published a Latin translation of Diophantus's "Arithmetica" and included his own research in it as commentary.

### M. Mersenne (1588-1648)
A French Minim friar. He acted as an intermediary for correspondence between mathematicians in various places. The numbers named Mersenne numbers were actually conceived by Fermat.

### R. Descartes (1596-1650)
An original French philosopher. His representative works include "Discourse on the Method," "Geometry," and "The World" (Cosmology).

### P. de Fermat (1601-1665)
A representative French mathematician of the 17th century. He laid the foundations for analytic geometry, probability theory, and differential calculus. He is also the founder of number theory.

### G. P. de Roberval (1602-1675)
A French mathematician. He was almost the only person to hold a professorship as a mathematician at the time, and is known for his research on the cycloid, among other things.

### J. Wallis (1616-1703)
An English mathematician. In his major work "Arithmetica Infinitorum," he gave mathematical form to the concept of limits, contributing to the foundations of calculus.

### W. Brouncker (1620-1684)
An Irish peer. He was the first President of the Royal Society. A mathematics enthusiast, he interacted with Wallis and others.

### B. Pascal (1623-1662)
A French philosopher known for his study of probability theory and fluids. He later joined the Jansenists and engaged in religious activities. "Pensées" is a work from that period.

### I. Newton (1642-1727)
An English physicist, mathematician, and astronomer. He founded differential and integral calculus independently of Leibniz and laid the foundation for classical mechanics. His masterpiece "Principia" is one of the most important books in the history of science. He is also known for discovering the law of universal gravitation.

### G. W. Leibniz (1646-1716)
A German philosopher and mathematician. He founded differential and integral calculus independently of Newton and devised the calculus notation we use today (such as dy/dx and ∫). He also contributed to the study of the binary numeral system and the formalization of logic.

### L. Euler (1707-1783)
The king of mathematics born in Switzerland. He made achievements in all possible areas of mathematics. More than 700 papers and 45 books of his remain.

### J. L. Lagrange (1736-1813)
An Italian-born French mathematician. He succeeded Euler as the Director of Mathematics at the Prussian Academy of Sciences in Berlin. He made great achievements in number theory and analytical mechanics.

### A. M. Legendre (1752-1833)
A French mathematician. He left numerous achievements in number theory, elliptic integrals, and the problem of gravitational attraction. The term "number theory" (Théorie des Nombres) is derived from his major work of the same name.

### C. F. Gauss (1777-1855)
The king of mathematics born in Germany. Known for his research in number theory, non-Euclidean geometry, geodesy, hypergeometric series, surface theory, and geomagnetism. A perfectionist, it is said he would not publish his results unless they were of such high perfection that the scaffolding was invisible. His main work is "Disquisitiones Arithmeticae".

### A. L. Cauchy (1789-1857)
A French mathematician. Prolific and with many achievements in theoretical physics and mathematics. It is said that he is as highly respected in France as Gauss is.

### G. Lamé (1795-1870)
A French mathematician. Contributed to the theory of elasticity, heat conduction, and more.

### N. H. Abel (1802-1829)
A Norwegian mathematician. He made outstanding achievements such as proving the impossibility of solving the general quintic equation, the theory of elliptic functions, and the theory of Abelian integrals, but died at a young age.

### C. G. J. Jacobi (1804-1851)
A Jewish-German mathematician. Left major achievements in the theory of elliptic functions, theta functions, differential equations, and the calculus of variations. He introduced Abel's achievements to the world. He is also known for rejecting pragmatism, stating that the purpose of science is "the honor of the human spirit."

### J. Liouville (1809-1882)
A French mathematician. Contributed to differential equations, transcendental number theory, and function theory.

### E. E. Kummer (1810-1893)
A German mathematician. Achieved great results by applying the number theory of cyclotomic fields to Fermat's Last Theorem.

### É. Galois (1811-1832)
A French mathematician. Clarified Abel's theorem that equations of degree 5 or higher cannot generally be solved algebraically by introducing the concept of a group. He died young in a duel, which is now known to have been sparked by a trivial dispute over a woman.

### K. Weierstrass (1815-1897)
A German mathematician. He is called the "father of modern analysis" for introducing the ε-δ (epsilon-delta) logical formulation to provide a rigorous foundation for calculus, which had previously relied on intuition. He also left enormous achievements in the theory of elliptic functions and complex analysis.

### L. Kronecker (1823-1891)
A Jewish-German mathematician. Contributed to the theory of elliptic functions, number theory, and more. Also known for advocating the arithmetical foundation of mathematics, but his highly subjective personality, unreasonable criticism of his University of Berlin colleague Weierstrass, and obstruction of Cantor's employment have cast a negative image on his life.

### B. Riemann (1826-1866)
A German mathematician. Made great achievements related to the very foundations of mathematics, such as the concept of Riemann surfaces in the theory of complex functions and Riemannian geometry.

### G. Cantor (1845-1918)
A German mathematician. The founder of set theory, he proved that there is a hierarchy (cardinality) to the sizes of infinite sets and proposed the continuum hypothesis. His innovative theories were fiercely criticized by the mathematical community at the time, but were later highly praised by Hilbert and others.

### H. Poincaré (1854-1912)
A French mathematician known as the last universalist in mathematics. Left outstanding works in areas including automorphic function theory, non-Euclidean geometry, differential equations, celestial mechanics, and electrodynamics, as well as in scientific commentary.

### K. Hensel (1861-1941)
A German mathematician. A student of Kronecker. He introduced the theory of p-adic numbers (p-adic analysis) into number theory by applying function-theoretic methods.

### D. Hilbert (1862-1943)
A German mathematician. Left a massive footprint in every field of mathematics, including invariant theory, algebraic number theory, and the foundations of mathematics. The 23 problems he proposed at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris (1900) had a profound impact on the development of mathematics.

### A. E. Noether (1882-1935)
A German mathematician. Made extremely important contributions to abstract algebra (ring theory and ideal theory). Moreover, "Noether's theorem" in theoretical physics connects symmetry and conservation laws, earning her praise from Einstein as "the greatest female mathematician."

### S. Ramanujan (1887-1920)
An Indian mathematician. Researching mathematics autodidactically with almost no formal mathematical education, he discovered numerous astonishing formulas concerning pi and partition numbers through intuition and flashes of insight. Known as "The Magician of India", he gained fame through his joint research with G. H. Hardy, but died of illness at a young age.

### L. J. Mordell (1888-1972)
An American-born mathematician. His parents were immigrants from Lithuania. He has numerous papers on the theory of Diophantine equations, but Mordell's theorem in the theory of elliptic curves is especially famous. Weil extended this to the case of general algebraic curves, from which arithmetic algebraic geometry underwent explosive development.

### C. L. Siegel (1896-1981)
A German number theorist. Made numerous contributions to the analytic theory of numbers.

### H. Hasse (1898-1979)
A German mathematician. A student of Hensel, he contributed to the foundation and application of p-adic analysis. His work includes the development of class field theory and the study of L-functions of elliptic curves.

### J. von Neumann (1903-1957)
A Hungarian-born American mathematician. In addition to laying the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics, founding game theory, and studying cellular automata, he conceived the "stored-program concept" (von Neumann architecture), which is the basic structure of modern computers. Because of his overwhelming computational power and memory, he was dubbed a "demonic brain".

### A. Weil (1906-1998)
A French mathematician of Jewish descent. In his youth, he took an interest in Fermat's problem and, for the purpose of applying it to number theory, fundamentally reviewed and strictly founded algebraic geometry. Also known for his writings on the history of mathematics.

### K. Gödel (1906-1978)
An Austrian-born logician and mathematician. The "incompleteness theorems" he published in 1931 showed that in any consistent formal axiomatic system, there are propositions that can neither be proved nor disproved, bringing an end to Hilbert's program and delivering a fundamental shock to the foundations of mathematics.

### A. Turing (1912-1954)
An English mathematician and cryptanalyst. He formalized the concept of computation by devising the "Turing machine" and is called the father of modern computer science and artificial intelligence. During World War II, he made enormous contributions to cracking the German Enigma code.

### A. Grothendieck (1928-2014)
A French mathematician. By introducing scheme theory, topos theory, and more, he radically rewrote algebraic geometry in the latter half of the 20th century. His methods of colossal abstraction had an immeasurable impact on modern mathematics as a whole, but he later suddenly retreated from the mathematical community.

### A. Baker (1939-)
An English number theorist. He was awarded the Fields Medal along with Heisuke Hironaka for his contributions to transcendental number theory.

### A. Wiles (1953-)
An English-born mathematician. He made major contributions through his research on Iwasawa's main conjecture, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and others. In 1995, he completely proved Fermat's Last Theorem, establishing an immortal monument in the history of mathematics.

### G. Faltings (1954-)
A German mathematician. He proved the Mordell conjecture in 1983 and was awarded the Fields Medal.
