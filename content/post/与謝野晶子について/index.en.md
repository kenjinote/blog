## 🔹 Person Overview

Akiko Yosano (1878-1942) was a Japanese poet, author, and thinker who was active from the Meiji through the early Showa periods. She is especially known for her passionate and unique tanka poems, and is a figure who opened new horizons for female independence and expressions of romance in modern Japanese literature.

* **Real Name**: Sho Yosano (maiden name: Otori)
* **Birthplace**: Sakai (Sakai City, Osaka Prefecture)
* **Husband**: Tekkan Yosano (tanka poet, poet)
* **Main Active Period**: Late Meiji to Early Showa periods
* **Characteristics**:

    * Garnered attention for tanka poems that frankly expressed a woman's romantic feelings
    * Held feminist ideals and promoted female education and independence
    * Also demonstrated a pacifist stance criticizing the Russo-Japanese War
    * Core member of the literary magazine "Myojo"

---

## 🔹 Representative Works, Synopses, and Characteristics

### 1. "Midaregami" (Tangled Hair) (1901)

* **Overview**: Her debut poetry collection. Published in her early 20s, it delivered a major shock to the Japanese literary world.
* **Characteristics**: Frankly expressed romantic feelings and physical desire from a female perspective.
* **Famous Poem**:

> Without even touching
> the hot blood of my soft skin
> aren't you lonely, you who preach the Way?

→ It became a topic of discussion for expressing a view of romance considered bold under the moral values of the time, in a woman's own words.

---

### 2. "Kimi Shinitamou koto nakare" (Thou Shalt Not Die) (1904)

* **Format**: Poem (free verse, not tanka)
* **Content**: An anti-war poem addressed to her younger brother deployed in the Russo-Japanese War.
* **Excerpt**:

> Thou shalt not die,
> As you were born last,
> Our mother wept bitterly
> While wringing your sleeves...

* **Significance**: Groundbreaking in that it appealed for anti-war sentiments from the perspective of a woman mourning her brother's potential death, amidst a strong trend of glorifying war at the time.

---

## 🔹 Influence and Evaluation

* Conducted socially advanced activities such as women's liberation, pacifism, and the popularization of education.
* Devoted her later years to women's education and was involved in the establishment of women's universities (e.g., Bunka Gakuin).
* One of the most important female intellectuals and literary figures in pre-war Japan.
