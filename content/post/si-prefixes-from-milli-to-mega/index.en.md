# 1. Introduction: What are SI Prefixes?

The world we live in spans an incomprehensible range of scales, from the vast expanses of the universe to the infinitesimally small realm of subatomic particles. To easily express such extremely large or small numbers, the International System of Units (SI) defines "SI prefixes."

For instance, calling 1,000 meters "1 kilometer" or 0.001 grams "1 milligram" allows us to avoid writing out long strings of zeros, helping us intuitively grasp the scale of a measurement.

At the 27th General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) in 2022, new SI prefixes were added for the first time in 31 years. This was driven by the explosive growth of global data and advancements in scientific research. The new prefixes are "Ronna" and "Quetta" for the macroscopic scale, and "Ronto" and "Quecto" for the microscopic scale.

## 2. Everyday Scales: From Milli to Kilo

First, let's look at the familiar prefixes we use most in our daily lives.

- **milli (10^-3)**: One-thousandth. 1 millimeter is about the diameter of a raindrop or an ant.
- **centi (10^-2)**: One-hundredth. 1 centimeter represents the width of a finger, a standard reference in our everyday scale.
- **deci (10^-1)**: One-tenth. Used occasionally, such as in deciliters, but less common than milli or centi.
- **kilo (10^3)**: One thousand times. 1 kilometer is a 15-minute walk, and 1 kilogram is the weight of a liter of milk.

These units accurately describe the life-sized world that humans can directly interact with.

## 3. The Digital Society and the Macro World: Mega to Quetta

With technological advancement, the amount of data and energy we handle has dramatically increased.

- **mega (10^6)**: One million times. Megabytes of image data or megapixels in cameras.
- **giga (10^9)**: One billion times. Gigabytes represent smartphone data capacities, a very common term today.
- **tera (10^12)**: One trillion times. Mainstream hard drives and SSDs now offer terabyte-class capacities.
- **peta (10^15) & exa (10^18)**: Used for supercomputer calculation speeds (petaflops) and global data generation (exabytes).
- **zetta (10^21) & yotta (10^24)**: The Earth's mass is about 6,000 yottagrams (6x10^24 g).

And the new additions from 2022:

- **ronna (10^27)**: Ideal for expressing the mass of planets like Jupiter (approx. 1.9 ronnagrams).
- **quetta (10^30)**: The Sun's mass is about 2,000 quettagrams. It's also expected to represent total future digital data.

## 4. The Invisible Micro World: Micro to Quecto

Science continues to dive deeper into the microscopic world of molecules, atoms, and subatomic particles.

- **micro (10^-6)**: One-millionth. The size of cells or particulate matter like PM2.5.
- **nano (10^-9)**: One-billionth. The width of DNA (about 2 nanometers) or advanced semiconductor manufacturing processes.
- **pico (10^-12)**: One-trillionth. Describes atomic sizes (a hydrogen atom's radius is about 50 picometers).
- **femto (10^-15)**: The size of atomic nuclei or the duration of ultrashort pulse lasers (femtosecond lasers).
- **atto (10^-18), zepto (10^-21), yocto (10^-24)**: Used in quantum mechanics and particle physics. A proton's mass is about 1.7 yoctograms.

The newly added prefixes to describe even smaller scales are:

- **ronto (10^-27)**: Represents extreme lightness, such as the mass of an electron (approx. 0.9 rontograms).
- **quecto (10^-30)**: The smallest prefix currently defined by humanity.

## 5. Conclusion: Units Expanding Human Vision

From milli to mega, and on to quetta and quecto, the expansion of SI prefixes is more than just adding names. It signifies the broadening of the universe that we can recognize, measure, and understand.
